## Population mutant (`same_board`) — the registered mechanism test

Both seats forced onto one virus board, so the only thing separating them is turn order. **Registered prediction: P(seat 0 wins) = 1.000.**

Measured: **P(seat 0 wins) = 0.4600** over 100 seeds (200 games).

**PREDICTION FAILED AS SPECIFIED.** Registered 1.000, measured 0.4600 (CI [0.36, 0.56]). Logged as a failure, not reinterpreted.

**Diagnosis — the PREDICATE was under-specified; the MECHANISM survives.** `same_board` equalises the virus boards but does not reach the exact-tie limit the prediction was about: garbage column phase is keyed on the GLOBAL volley ordinal (`seed*7919 + vol` in `play_match`), so the two seats take garbage in different columns and diverge from the first release onward. Measured: of 200 same_board games, **zero had zero releases** (mean 26.3 releases/game) — not one was ever in the tie limit.

Severing the attack channel as well (`garbage=False`, boards still identical) leaves turn order as the only asymmetry. Measured over 100 seeds: **P(seat 0 wins) = 1.0000** (100/100), with 0 games having any release.

**The first-mover mechanism is therefore real and total in the true tie limit**: both seats play identical moves, seat 0 moves first, and its terminal condition is detected before seat 1 gets its move that round. The registered prediction named the right mechanism and the wrong CONDITION for reaching it.

⚠ **It does not translate into a measurable seat bias in real play.** The mirror arm's CI contains 0.5. A structural advantage that is total under identical boards is swamped once board or garbage variation is present. Both statements hold at once: the mechanism exists, and its effect size in the regime actually played is not distinguishable from zero.
