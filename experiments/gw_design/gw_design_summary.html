Dr. Mario RL · task #117 · branch gw-design · 2026-08-18

The garbage window is a one-to-three searches budget, not a rollout budget.

The coprocessor is idle for 100% of every garbage drop. Costing that window in copro cycles against 1,500 measured decision costs settles what can actually run there — and rules out the thing the lane was hoping for.

Verdict

Porting H12 into the window is dead, and not marginally — it needs up to 300 champion decisions per intervention against a window that holds at most 5.3. What is newly affordable is a 2-candidate, one-ply deepening of the top-2 tie, which fits on 52.4% of releases. The decisive screen costs about $4 and needs no hardware. Recommendation: build nothing yet, run that screen.

✅ The lane's motivating measurement is confirmed, in every regime. An earlier version of this page said the 50.5% pre-vs-post-garbage argmax flip was un-reproducible and out-of-regime. Both claims were wrong — the rigs existed all along, in the session scratchpad where no repository search reaches. All three have now run: 50.5% at low fill, 53.2% at 45–60% fill, 66.3% on real kill-game boards at stack 13–16, against a 2% testability floor. The effect is strongest in the near-death regime this lane targets.

⚠ Retraction: the window does not shrink where the danger is

Earlier drafts carried “the window is shortest exactly in the near-death regime” as the lane's central pessimism. Measured on 125 real kill-game boards, it is false. That claim came from flat synthetic stacks, where every column has the same height. Real near-death boards are towers: the tallest column has median height 15, but the window is set by the shallowest garbage-hit column — and garbage lands in spread columns, so it almost always finds a low one.

Shallowest-hit height has median 4, giving a near-death window of median 200 frames = 3.33 s, not the 0.93 s implied by the synthetic. Only 3.2% of real death boards land in the short-window shape. So the compute is abundant and most valuable in the same regime — ~3.3 s of window alongside the highest flip rate measured anywhere (66.3%).

The largest lever: truncation is nearly free

Depth 3 is best-first over a depth-1 ranking — it commits the shallow favourite immediately and spends the rest confirming. So a truncated search is usually the same search. The curve is already measured on 69 real boards on the shipped arm, and combining it with the budget table turns a marginal feature into a comfortable one at the p90 cost that actually binds.

The go/no-go arithmetic therefore uses the untruncated 52.4% and treats any lift from truncation as upside.

How big is the dose?

A budget says what can run; whether an experiment can detect it depends on how often it fires. Both halves are measurable from banked data, and the answer is the encouraging part of this document.

13.87% of plies are post-garbage decisions measured · n=1500
18.88% of plies have an exact top-2 champion-value tie measured · 9,000 games
0.48% of plies actually trigger, after de-duplication and affordability corrected · see below
1.98% H12's own accepted-flip dose, for comparison measured · 9,000 games

⚠ Retraction: 87% of “exact top-2 ties” are the same physical placement

An earlier version of this page put the trigger population at 1.37–1.66% of plies and called it “the same order as H12's 1.98% — the encouraging part of this document”. That was wrong, and it was caught while gating the screen, before the registered run.

A Dr. Mario capsule is a double (both halves the same colour) one time in three, and a double is symmetric under 180°: orientations 0 and 2 are the same placement, as are 1 and 3. So the action space collapses from 32 to 16 and every placement appears twice with exactly equal value. Measured over 359 post-garbage plies: at a raw top-2 tie the two candidates produce a literally identical board 87.1% of the time, with identical legal-move counts 100% of the time. A binary comparator over the raw top-2 is comparing a board with itself, and no amount of deepening can discriminate.

De-duplicating candidates by resulting board shrinks the tie population 7.53× (6.86% → 0.911% of plies), giving a corrected trigger population of ~0.48% — 4× smaller than H12's dose, not comparable to it. Power is now a live risk rather than a reassurance. H12 itself is not affected: identical candidates yield identical fork labels, so its θ-margin gate rejects them automatically. What was overstated is the tie count used as a dose statistic — which is what I had used.

⚠ And size is still not dose. Per the distill lane's law, dose must be weighted by selection quality — its safe linear rule fired at a usable rate but carried only 2.7% of H12's effective dose, because precision fell as fast as firing rose. One extra ply is a far weaker discriminator than a 15-pill rollout, so per-flip quality remains unknown and is exactly what the screen exists to measure.

A free tempo win falls out of this, unrelated to the lane. For a double capsule the mirrored orientations are the same placement but not the same cost to reach: the executor is CCW-only, so orient 1 costs three rotations from spawn where orient 3 costs one. Canonicalising to the cheapest-to-reach member is a pure tempo gain with provably zero board effect.

★ Here the truncation curve needs no transfer assumption. It was measured for the full depth-3 root search and only assumed to carry to the 2-candidate deepening — but the base re-search is that full root search, same object and same best-first structure. For the mandatory step this is measurement, not extrapolation; the assumption stays confined to the optional extras. h = 16 is the only write-off, and that board is already lost — the garbage overwrites row 0 and the next spawn tops out.

And the regime this rescues is rarer than it looks, which is better news still. Since only 3.2% of real death boards have a short window, the primary near-death story is not truncation at all — it is that a complete re-search fits, in ~3.3 s, at a 66.3% flip rate. Truncation is tail insurance for the remainder.

⚠ It does force a design change. Abandon-whole pre-emption is the wrong default above h = 13, where it would discard a result worth 90–100% fidelity. The rule becomes height-dependent: abandon below h = 13, adopt at ≥ 80% completion above it — gated on a completion counter, never a timer, behind the same seqlock, with a killed mutant where adopting below the threshold must reproduce the stale-move signature. Adopting partials is the pair-latch failure mode by another name.

Three design constraints that fall out of the numbers

mandatory first step · confirmed

Search the post-garbage board, or don't bother

Half of all argmax decisions flip between the pre- and post-garbage board, so letting the existing search run longer on the stale board is wasted work half the time. Measured in three regimes: 50.5% low fill, 53.2% at 45–60% fill, 66.3% near-death. It holds everywhere, and hardest where the window is shortest.

atomicity

Publish the result as one object, never field-by-field

The shipped pair-latch defect is exactly a two-field result sampled across time — worth −15.8pp clear rate. The driver runs two hooks per frame, so a split publish recreates it by default. A seqlock makes the bad pair unrepresentable for ~12 instructions.

pre-emption

Abandon whole; degrade to the certified move

Write the valid flag only on completion. If the spawn edge lands first, re-GO exactly as today — which also makes the OFF path provably identical. Abandonment isn't free: it disarms the slam for that capsule.

How to price it, given that the farm can't see time

The co-sim farm has no time axis, and its baseline already receives the settled post-garbage board for free. The way through is to stop asking it a joint question:

shippable effect = P(compute completes | h) × value(move produced | compute granted)

The left factor is pure timing — the table above answers it. The right factor has no timing content, so the farm can measure it honestly by granting the compute unconditionally. The farm's prestart bias is common-mode across both arms here and cancels — which is precisely why this lane is priceable where DRPRESTART was not. Report the right factor stratified by h, never pooled, so the product can be formed height by height.

Risk register

  • blocking

    NMI overrun. Two trace-proven hazards fire when a hook runs long; mechanism 2 is confirmed on ship bytes as the root cause of the 2026-08-09 field crash. DRPRESTART's release-edge spike already eats 40% of a frame, and any hook-lengthening flag re-arms it. The fix spec must land before this feature — and the two NMI fixes brick each other, so gate the combination.

  • hard limit

    Pocket fit: 18,262 / 18,480 ALMs = 99%, ~218 spare — inside fitter-seed noise on that device. This feature must cost zero ALMs on Pocket. The design satisfies that by construction; any variant needing new RTL is MiSTer-only and cannot ship to the rematch venue. MiSTer has 11% free.

  • hard limit

    Flag interactions. DRPRESTART × DRTUCK is a wedge pair — 9 pills and a hang, with neither flag at fault alone. A garbage-window knob extends DRPRESTART and inherits it. Since a tuck CvC cart must ship DRPRESTART=0, this feature and the tuck executor cannot coexist on that cart line at all. That is a product fork, not a build detail.

  • trade-off

    Firmware placement costs A/B agility. Putting the extras in copro firmware keeps host-side hook length unchanged — the right call for NMI safety — but $readmemh resolves at synthesis, so every arm is a full ~40 min compile, and the cart can neither see nor verify which core it is plugged into. Mitigation worth doing regardless: a firmware capability byte the cart reads at init.

  • evidence

    The number the lane rests on cannot currently be shown. The 50.5% flip has no reproducible artifact in any of the 30 worktrees — it survives only as prose plus one downstream citation — and every board behind it was under 45% fill, excluding the regime the lane targets. Re-deriving it with a committed rig is folded into the screen. The release-height distribution here is separately thin: n=208 from 10 games, 4–8 observations in the decisive cells, so read that column as a scale, not a rate.

  • measurement

    A 3× conflict over search cost, raised in review and resolved. A competing figure of 150 frames per warm search would have killed the lane. It traces to a hook-counted constant authored to mean ~60 frames under a 5-hooks/frame assumption; a later audit corrected the rate to 2/frame, fixed the surrounding prose, and left the constant — so it now reads 2.5× long. Four independent measurements (31.6 / 34 / 57.2 / 60 frames) agree with the original intent. Knock-on: the DRSLAM arming threshold was tuned against that same stale constant.
